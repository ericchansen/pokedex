# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary user is a knowledgeable Pokémon player managing a personal collection across Pokémon HOME, Pokémon Champions, and classic competitive games. They work with large, information-dense inventories and need to move quickly between spatial box organization, analytical inventory views, reusable competitive Builds, and battle Teams.

## Product Purpose

Pokémon HOME Tracker is a local-first tool for maintaining an accurate model of a real Pokémon collection. It mirrors HOME storage, distinguishes owned Instances from reusable competitive Builds, supports team composition, and makes form, game-availability, placement, and training state visible without forcing the user into separate disconnected workflows.

Success means the tracker remains trustworthy at collection scale: data is precise, navigation is fast, editing is consistent, and Pokémon—not application chrome—remain the focus.

## Brand Personality

Focused, nostalgic, precise. The product should feel native to an experienced Pokémon player's workflow: recognizable and characterful without becoming childish, disciplined without becoming sterile, and compact without becoming cryptic.

## Anti-references

The interface must not resemble a sterile enterprise administration tool. Avoid generic corporate dashboard patterns, detached business terminology, excessive empty space, and chrome that makes the collection feel secondary. Familiar product affordances are welcome, but they should retain Pokémon-specific context and visual identity.

## Design Principles

1. **Pokémon are the interface.** Sprites, species, forms, boxes, Builds, and Teams carry the hierarchy; chrome stays compact and supporting.
2. **Earn trust through precision.** Never imply that an unknown form, placement, or state is confirmed. Make ambiguity and mismatches explicit.
3. **Honor expert density.** Present large collections efficiently, preserve fast scanning, and reveal detail progressively rather than diluting the workflow.
4. **Use nostalgia as texture, not friction.** Pixel-era cues and Pokémon-native assets may add character, but standard controls and readable data remain familiar.
5. **One concept, one behavior.** Shared entities and actions should look and work consistently across Boxes, Inventory, Builds, Teams, and the reusable viewer/editor.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Preserve the application's current accessibility behavior and avoid regressions as the interface evolves. No additional formal conformance target is currently required.
